SheepGuard

UI / UX Design

Scam Detection App

Concept Project

Overview

Scam detection app inspired by “Wolf in Sheep’s Clothing”.

SheepGuard is a mobile concept that helps users quickly judge whether a call or message is safe, suspicious, or manipulative. Instead of relying on dense security language, the experience translates scam detection into a more intuitive visual story.

The project turns the fable of “the wolf in sheep’s clothing” into a product metaphor: harmless appearances can hide danger, and interface feedback should make that risk instantly legible.

Principle 01

Clarity before complexity.

Every result should answer the user’s immediate question first: is this safe, suspicious, or dangerous?

Principle 02

Emotion as signal.

The sheep-to-wolf metaphor turns hidden danger into a visual response users can feel without reading paragraphs.

Principle 03

Learning through use.

The app does not only warn users in the moment; it gradually teaches them how scam patterns work.

Audience + Journey

Why I needed a journey map.

I used it to find where users feel most uncertain and where clear trust signals and simple actions matter most.

Visual Metaphor

Why I used a visual metaphor.

It makes hidden risk instantly recognizable, helping users react before reading detailed warnings.

Design System

Flow

Why I needed a clear flow.

It connects scanning, call detection, learning, and practice so users can move quickly from risk to action.

Feature 01

Scan Message

Users can paste suspicious content or upload a screenshot, then receive a result that balances immediate feedback with a short explanation.

Feature 02

AI Call Detector

A configurable protection level controls how proactive the app becomes when unfamiliar callers or risky patterns appear.

Feature 03

Learn + Simulate

Educational modules and practice interactions help users build pattern recognition instead of depending on alerts forever.

Prototype

Why I built the prototype.

It let me test whether the full experience felt calm, clear, and decisive.

Interaction Logic

Why protection levels matter.

They let users choose how actively AI should intervene, matching different comfort and risk levels.

Warning State

Why the wolf reveal matters.

It turns uncertain risk into an immediate, memorable warning before users read the details.

Reflection

Why this direction mattered.

It showed that a familiar metaphor can make digital safety easier to understand and act on.
